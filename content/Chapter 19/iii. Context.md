## Locating Gog: Magog, Meshech, and Tubal in the Ancient World

The question of where Ezekiel's Gog actually comes from has produced one of the more contested geographic debates in prophecy studies, and the contest has a traceable modern history.

The identification of Gog and Magog with Russia rests substantially on nineteenth-century philological work, most associated with the Hebrew lexicographer Wilhelm Gesenius, who proposed that "Rosh" — a word appearing in some translations of Ezekiel 38:2 as "chief prince" (from the Hebrew *rosh*, "head" or "chief") — should instead be read as a proper name referring to a people identifiable with the Rus, the medieval forerunners of the Russian state. Building on that reading, "Meshech" and "Tubal" were linked by sound association to Moscow and Tobolsk, Russian place names. This reading gained enormous popularity in twentieth-century prophecy literature, particularly during the Cold War, when identifying Gog with the Soviet Union carried obvious contemporary resonance. The association persists widely in popular prophecy teaching today, even though most scholars regard the Rosh-as-Russia connection and the Meshech/Moscow, Tubal/Tobolsk sound-alikes as etymologically unsupported — the place names in question did not exist, in anything like their modern form, at the time Ezekiel was writing, and sound-alike names across unrelated languages separated by well over two thousand years are not reliable evidence of common origin.

The alternative reading — locating Magog, Meshech, and Tubal in ancient Anatolia, the region of modern Turkey — rests on more direct evidence. Meshech and Tubal appear in Assyrian records as Mushku and Tabal, kingdoms and peoples situated in central and eastern Anatolia during the first millennium BC, and both are named alongside each other in inscriptions from the Neo-Assyrian period. Magog itself is harder to pin to a single site with the same precision, but its placement in the Table of Nations in Genesis 10 groups it among peoples generally associated with the same broader region — the descendants of Japheth settling in areas north and west of the Fertile Crescent. Some scholars have further proposed identifying Gog himself with Gyges, a seventh-century BC king of Lydia in western Anatolia known from Greek historical sources, though this identification remains a proposal rather than a settled conclusion. Taken together, the Anatolian identification enjoys considerably stronger textual and archaeological grounding than the Rosh-as-Russia reading, even though the latter remains far better known in popular prophecy circles.

## The Restrainer in 2 Thessalonians 2: A History of Interpretation

Few phrases in Pauline literature have generated as much interpretive disagreement as "the one who now holds it back" in 2 Thessalonians 2:6–7 — commonly called "the restrainer," from the Greek *katechon* (the restraining thing or principle) and *katechōn* (the restraining one, a person). Paul refers to the same reality first as a neuter force and then, a verse later, as a masculine person, which has itself fueled centuries of debate over whether one or two entities are in view.

The earliest and historically dominant Christian interpretation, going back to figures like Tertullian and Chrysostom in the patristic era, identified the restrainer with the Roman Empire and its emperor — the political order of the day, understood as the force holding back total lawlessness and chaos. This reading made intuitive sense to readers living under Roman rule and facing the possibility of its collapse, but it becomes considerably harder to sustain once the Roman Empire has, in fact, fallen and lawlessness has manifestly not culminated in the man of lawlessness's revealing.

Modern interpretation clusters around several competing proposals: the Holy Spirit, working through the church, restraining evil until the church's removal from the earth; human government in a more general sense, understood as an ongoing (rather than specifically Roman) restraining institution; and the view this study favors, built on the passage's own immediate context — Satan himself, or more precisely the present limits on Satan's authority prior to his final expulsion from heavenly access, described in Revelation 12. Each reading carries real interpretive consequences. The Holy-Spirit reading is the load-bearing wall beneath the pre-tribulation rapture position, since it requires the Spirit's personal presence to be physically withdrawn from earth — which in that framework requires the church, in whom the Spirit dwells, to be removed first. Readings that instead identify the restrainer with Satan's own currently limited authority avoid that requirement entirely, since Satan's removal from heavenly access in Revelation 12 does not depend on the church's prior removal from earth.

## Israel's Sacrificial Readiness: The Red Heifer Program in Real-World Context

The session's claim that Israel already possesses red heifer ashes and a consecrated altar, and could resume sacrifices within days of gaining Temple Mount access, reflects a real and closely watched effort — though the on-the-ground status is considerably more contested than a brief summary can convey, and it has shifted meaningfully even within the past few years.

In September 2022, five red heifer calves were flown from a Texas ranch to Israel, arranged through a Christian rancher and Israeli religious organizations including the Temple Institute, which has spent decades preparing priestly garments, vessels, and training for a resumed sacrificial system. The animals were raised at Shiloh under religious supervision, aged toward the point at which Jewish law would permit their use in the purification ritual described in Numbers 19 — a rite requiring an unblemished red heifer that has never borne a yoke, its ashes mixed with water to ceremonially purify anyone who would approach a future altar.

The subsequent history has been a repeated cycle of anticipation and disqualification rather than a clean success. Reports through 2024 and into 2025 described one or more of the original heifers developing disqualifying traits (non-red hairs, in Jewish legal terms) as they aged, and by August 2025 the Temple Institute publicly confirmed that all five of the original Texas-born heifers had been disqualified for use in the purification rite. A separate July 2025 ceremony, initially reported by some outlets as the actual qualifying sacrifice, was subsequently clarified by both the Temple Institute and Israel's nascent Sanhedrin to have been a rehearsal rather than a rite meeting the full biblical requirements — a clarification both bodies felt necessary given how quickly the initial reports spread. As of early 2026, several additional heifer candidates remain under observation, and the Temple Institute continues to describe daily sacrifice as one of roughly a dozen major steps still requiring completion, including formal approval from the Israeli government, which has shown limited present appetite for the step.

None of this undermines the session's core structural point — the sanctuary requirement is logistical rather than architectural, and a rebuilt Solomonic temple is not a prerequisite for resumed sacrifice. But the specific claim that qualifying ashes are already in hand is, as of the most recent reporting, unsettled rather than confirmed. This is a fast-moving, closely watched situation, and readers interested in its current status should look for the most recent reporting available.
