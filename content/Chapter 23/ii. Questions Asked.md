> *Questions raised during the session, with answers drawn from the teaching.*

---

### Q: Isn't it significant that the white horse rider has a bow and the red horse rider has a sword? Why two different weapons if it's the same figure?

The distinction is real, but it marks two different phases of the same campaign rather than two different people.

The bow is the weapon of the one who rides out to conquer. It signals reach and authorization — he has been given the means to subdue whoever stands in his way. The sword belongs to the phase after the conquest begins: peace taken from the earth, men killing each other, warfare at scale. A conqueror going out is not doing the same thing as a world at war, even though the second is the direct result of the first.

The larger question the objection raises is whether anyone else in this period is bringing war on a scale that would require a separate rider. Prophetically, there is no one. The Antichrist is the chief architect of every war in this period. There will be opposition to him, but he is the one winning, and he consolidates power continuously until the seventieth week runs out.

---

### Q: The Ezekiel 39 imagery is about God *disarming* him — striking the bow from his hand. Isn't that the opposite of the white horse rider, who is conquering effectively?

Yes, and that is exactly what makes the parallel useful. The two passages show the same figure with the same weapon at opposite ends of his career.

In Revelation 6, the bow is given as he rides out. He is effective because he has been authorized to be effective — the Lord has not taken the bow from his hand or the arrows from his grip. In Ezekiel 39, at the end, the bow is struck away and the arrows fall. That is the imagery of a man rendered powerless, and it occurs at Armageddon, immediately before he is destroyed.

The bow given and the bow taken are two ends of one story. He conquers for exactly as long as he is permitted to conquer, and not one day longer.

---

### Q: Why does it say "bow" and never mention arrows at all? Is that specific?

It is generic. If you send a man out to conquer, he has to have something to conquer with. The bow is standing in for the capacity to make war — an emblem of the authorization rather than an inventory of his equipment. Revelation does not describe the arrows because the point of the image is not the mechanics of archery. It is that he goes out armed, crowned as a victor, and he will not be ineffective. He will dominate everyone he comes into contact with.

---

### Q: Are the four horsemen four different versions of the Antichrist, or four different rulers?

Neither, exactly. They are four facets of one campaign.

The rider is nameless in every scene. The only things that change from seal to seal are the color of the horse and what that rider brings with him — conquest, war, famine, death. There is no differentiation of person in the text, only of effect.

---

### Q: Where does it actually say that all four riders are the Antichrist? Couldn't there just be four different evil kings doing four different things — the other "toe kings," for instance?

The text does not say it outright, which is a fair objection. It has to be established from Daniel.

Ask what prophetic warrant exists for multiple significant rulers during this period. There isn't any. Daniel describes one — and shows precisely how he gets there. Both of Daniel's parallel visions show ten kings arising first: the ten toes of iron and clay on the statue, and the ten horns on the fourth beast. Out of that group, the little horn rises and subdues three of the ten by force. The remaining seven hand their power over to him. He becomes king over all ten kingdoms.

That is the function of the ten kings in the prophetic record: they exist to transfer authority. Once that transfer is complete, they drop out of the narrative entirely. Daniel does not follow them further. So there is no ruler standing alongside the Antichrist as a peer during this period. There is a false prophet, but the false prophet works directly for him.

---

### Q: If it's the same rider throughout, then his name was Death and Hades all along — his name wouldn't change partway through, would it?

The riders are not named individuals with fixed identities. They are symbolic of the Antichrist and of what his arrival brings on the earth. The naming at the fourth seal is not a personal name being revealed; it is the effect being labeled. Death is the killing of human beings on a massive scale. Hades follows behind because death delivers those who die apart from God somewhere.

---

### Q: What is Hades?

Hades is the consequence of dying without the Lord. Hell. And hell, the lake of fire, and the abyss are all words for the same place — the place of eternal torment away from God. Different passages approach it with different vocabulary and from different angles, but they are describing one destination.

### Q: "A quart of wheat for a day's wages" — is that enough to feed a family, or one person?

Roughly one person's daily ration, depending on appetite. That is the force of the image. You work an entire day to eat for that day, and there is nothing left over for anyone else in the household. It describes ruinous inflation, not simple shortage.

---

### Q: Isn't the rider on the black horse literally the one bringing the famine? The living creatures keep saying "Come" — they're speaking to the rider.

The rider is the mechanism through which the story is being told rather than a figure personally distributing scarcity. But the underlying point stands: the Antichrist is the ultimate cause. War is his campaign, and famine is what war produces.

The "Come" is worth taking seriously in its own right, though — it is establishing something important. Authority in heaven is issuing a summons. The creatures are not spectators; they are giving permission and calling the rider forward. This is ordained. It is not something Satan is bringing about that catches God off guard and requires a response. It is the plan.

---

### Q: If God pours out his wrath on the earth, wouldn't that kill everyone left who doesn't believe in him?

Not everyone. Even though these events sound cataclysmic from where we stand, God is looking at each individual heart as his wrath is being poured out. He knows who will yet make a choice and who will not. That is why there are still living people on the earth to enter the millennial kingdom.

---

### Q: So God chooses 144,000 Jews to be spared from suffering, and none of them believe in him yet?

They are not believers at the point they are sealed. They become believers when the Lamb returns and they see him.

The distinction being made here is between knowing and believing, and it runs throughout Scripture. When Jesus encountered the man from the Gadarenes with a legion of demons, the demons addressed him as Son of God and asked whether he had come to torture them before the appointed time. They knew exactly who he was. That did not make him their Lord. Pharaoh understood perfectly well that he was under the hand of the God of Israel. He did not believe in him. You can acknowledge that someone exists, and even that he is who he says he is, without having submitted to him.

---

### Q: But in Revelation 6, the people hiding in the rocks know it's God. If they know, why can't they choose right at that moment?

They know, and they know they are on the wrong side. Recognition arrives, but it arrives as terror rather than repentance — they call for the mountains to fall on them rather than face the one they can now see.

As for whether the window has closed: for the people hiding in the rocks at the sixth seal, the answer is that repentance remains available. Everyone can repent up to one of two points — their own death, or the great white throne judgment at the end of the millennial kingdom. Those who survive into the millennial kingdom have a genuine choice to make, and some will make it there.

---

### Q: Are any of the 144,000 women?

No. Revelation 14:4 describes them as those who did not defile themselves with women and kept themselves pure. They are all men.

---

### Q: Twelve disciples, twelve tribes, twelve thousand from each. Is the number doing something?

Twelve is unmistakably a structural number in Scripture, and twelve thousand from each of the twelve tribes is clearly deliberate. Why twelve thousand specifically rather than some other multiple is not disclosed. That is what he said he would do, and it is enough to know that he said it.

---

### Q: They're not Jehovah's Witnesses, are they?

No. The 144,000 in Revelation 7 are explicitly identified as twelve thousand from each of the twelve tribes of Israel — ethnic Jews, sealed on the earth during the wrath of God, and standing with the Lamb on Mount Zion in Revelation 14. Any reading that transfers the number to a modern religious body is reading against the text.

---

### Q: Isn't the point of the millennial kingdom the fulfillment of God's Old Testament promises?

The covenant is the *reason* it happens. It is not the only purpose.

Somewhere between half and three quarters of the covenant promises God made to Abraham, to Moses, to David, and through Jesus have not yet been fulfilled in history. They will be fulfilled in the millennial kingdom: Israel's king on the throne, the nation at peace in the land, the full geographical inheritance actually possessed.

But the purpose that sits alongside that is decisional. The millennial kingdom is where Israel and the rest of the nations come to terms with Jesus as King of kings and Lord of lords and make their final decision. Those who enter it have a choice to make, exactly as we do now — with one significant difference in their favor. They will watch a righteous king ruling the earth from Jerusalem and executing perfect judgment. That is not a condition any of us have had.

---

### Q: What happens to the rest of the Jews who aren't among the 144,000?

If they survive, they enter the millennial kingdom. Survival, not prior belief, is the condition for entering it — the millennial kingdom is not for the church. Those who believe at the time of the Lord's return are resurrected and will reign with him. The millennial kingdom exists for those still living on the earth.

---

### Q: Will there still be death in the millennial kingdom? Babies born?

Yes to both. Some passages indicate that death still occurs, but lifespans are dramatically extended. There will still be sin and there will still be rebellion — what will not be present is Satan, who is bound for the duration. Free will does not change. That is the constant across every era of the story.

Detail on the millennial kingdom is genuinely limited in Scripture. Conditions are better than the present earth — closer to a paradise — but it is not the final state. The new heaven and new earth are still to come after it.

---

### Q: They're still going to make sacrifices during the millennial kingdom, with Jesus physically present? Why would he allow that when he is the Lamb?

That is how the Lord has set it up. Ezekiel 40–48 describes a temple, a functioning sacrificial system, and the glory of the Lord filling it. They are offering sacrifices to their king, in his presence, in his house.

They will know who he is. The moment he returns to the earth, Israel will know that they missed their own sign.

---

### Q: Isn't there something in Zechariah about the nations having to come to Jerusalem every year or they get no rain?

Zechariah 14. Every year during the Feast of Tabernacles — the September–October window on the calendar — every nation is required to send pilgrims up to Jerusalem to take part in the celebration. Any nation that refuses receives no rain on its land, the same instrument of judgment used in Elijah's day.

The principle it establishes is that rebellion still has consequences in the millennial kingdom. If a people rebels against their king, there are results. What is different is that the king is physically present and the consequence is immediate and unmistakable.
