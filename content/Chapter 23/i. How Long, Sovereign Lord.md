> *"When he opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slain because of the word of God and the testimony they had maintained. They called out in a loud voice, 'How long, Sovereign Lord, holy and true, until you judge the inhabitants of the earth and avenge our blood?'"*
> — Revelation 6:9–10

## Overview

Revelation 6 and 7 are where the pieces finally lock together. Everything this study has assembled — Daniel's four kingdoms and his seventy sevens, Ezekiel's oracles against Gog, Matthew 24, Paul's letters to the Thessalonians — has been describing the same seven-year period from different angles and in different imagery. Here, in the breaking of seven seals, the sequence is laid out in order, and the question that has hung over the whole study gets an answer: where, exactly, does the church's rescue fall inside the end?

The answer this chapter arrives at is not comfortable, but it is clear. The first six seals run from the [[Glossary#Antichrist|Antichrist]]'s arrival through the darkening of the sun, moon, and stars. The martyrs of the [[Glossary#Great Tribulation|great tribulation]] are already under the altar by the fifth seal. And before the seventh seal is ever broken, the Lord has returned, gathered his church, and sealed a hundred and forty-four thousand out of Israel against what comes next. The seven trumpets and the seven bowls — the [[Glossary#Wrath of God|wrath of God]] proper — are on the far side of that gathering.

Underneath the sequence is a pastoral point that matters more than the chronology. The martyrs cry out *how long*, and they are not given a date. They are given a robe and told to wait. The reason the Lord spends a quarter of Scripture's prophetic material telling his people exactly how this ends is that knowing the plan is what makes the waiting survivable.

---

## 1. One Picture, Many Vantage Points

Before opening the seals, a methodological point governs everything that follows. The prophets are not describing different sets of events. Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah, Zechariah, Joel, Matthew 24, Paul, and John are all describing the *same* events — from different centuries, different vantage points, and through different imagery.

That is why the correlations are so tight. It is not coincidence, and it is not a literary borrowing. It is the same truth being shown from different sides.

Practically, this means no single passage contains the whole thing. Each one supplies a piece — this one gives the left edge of the frame, that one gives the top. Assembled, the picture becomes clear. Read in isolation, any one of them stays partial. Revelation is where the last pieces go in, which is why it cannot be interpreted apart from what came before it and why what came before it stays incomplete without Revelation.

> **Key Principle:** No single prophetic passage contains the entire picture. The prophets describe the same events through different visions and different imagery, and the picture only becomes clear when they are stitched together.

*(A side-by-side mapping of Matthew 24, Daniel, and Revelation 6 across a single timeline will be the subject of the next session.)*

## 2. The Seals, the Lamb, and the Four Living Creatures

The scene continues directly from the throne room. The Lamb who was found worthy to take the scroll now begins to break it open. Each of the first four seals is announced by one of the [[Glossary#Four Living Creatures|four living creatures]] — the same creatures Ezekiel describes in Ezekiel 1, with the faces of a man, a lion, an ox, and an eagle.

The announcement is a single word: *Come.*

That word is doing more work than it appears to. These are not spectators narrating events. They are issuing a summons. The rider does not ride until he is called out, and he is called out only after the Lamb has broken the seal that holds him. Every catastrophe in this chapter arrives on invitation, from a throne, in an order set in heaven.

The first four seals are traditionally called the [[Glossary#Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse|Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse]]. Each involves a horse of a distinct color and a rider. The riders are never named — with one exception at the fourth seal — and what identifies each of them is not a name but an authorization: what he has been given, and what he is permitted to do.

---

## 3. The First Seal: The Rider on the White Horse

*"I looked, and there before me was a white horse! Its rider held a bow, and he was given a crown, and he rode out as a conqueror bent on conquest."* (Revelation 6:2)

Every instinct trained by Western storytelling reads a white horse as the hero. Purity. The rescuer arriving to save the world. That instinct is exactly what the imagery is exploiting.

What identifies this rider is not the color of his horse but what he carries and what he does. He holds a bow. He is *given* a crown — the passive verb matters, and it will recur through all four seals. And he rides out as a conqueror bent on conquest.

The crown is a specific one. The Greek is *stephanos*, not *diadēma*. A *diadēma* is the crown of inherited royalty. A [[Glossary#Victor's Crown (Stephanos)|*stephanos*]] is the victor's crown — the laurel wreath awarded to the winner of an athletic contest in the Greek games. This is not the crown of a man born to rule. It is the crown of a man who won.

Over time, the wreath drifted from a symbol of victory to a symbol of peace, which is precisely the ambiguity the first seal trades on. The rider arrives looking like the one who has finally solved what no one else could.

Set that against what the study already established from Daniel 9. The seven-year period does not begin with a war. It begins with a covenant — a peace arrangement confirmed with many, brought by the Antichrist. The question that follows is a practical one: how do you bring peace to a region that does not want peace? There are only two mechanisms. You are so powerful that no one dares cross you, or you are so legitimate that everyone wants to bow. An aura of power, or actual power exercised until the opposition folds. In the Middle East, without power of one kind or the other, it cannot be done.

So the rider goes out with a peace crown and a weapon, and the text tells you what he does with them: he conquers. Which means there is opposition, and it means there will be war. The peace is real enough to be signed and forced enough to be temporary.

---

## 4. The Four Horsemen and the Four Birth Pains

The four horsemen correspond directly to the four [[Glossary#Birth Pains (Eschatological)|birth pains]] Jesus lists in Matthew 24: the arrival of false messiahs and the deceiver, wars and rumors of wars, famine and scarcity as nation rises against nation, and death on a scale the world has not seen. Two symbol sets, two vocabularies, one sequence.

Matthew's framing adds something Revelation does not state outright. Birth pains do two things as labor progresses: they come closer together, and they get more intense. Charted, it is a rising wave.

That is why the presence of antichrist figures, wars, famines, and internal rebellions throughout the last two thousand years is not an argument against this reading. Those have been happening since the first century. They are the early contractions — small, spread out, real. What Matthew 24 describes and what Revelation 6 opens is the same wave at its peak. The period of the seals is not the introduction of these things. It is their maximum.

---

## 5. Whose Timing Is This?

The most important question in the chapter is not *who is the rider*. It is *who is running this*.

The natural assumption is that the Antichrist is executing his own plan on his own schedule, or that Satan is. Neither is what the text shows. Every one of these scenes begins the same way: the Lamb opens a seal. Nothing rides until the Lamb breaks the seal and the living creature says *come*.

The image that fits is a guard dog on a leash. The animal is genuinely dangerous and genuinely wants to do what it does. But it goes when the hand holding the leash lets it go, and not before.

This is not Satan launching an offensive that catches God off guard and forces a response. This is the Lord releasing an agent, at a time of his choosing, in a pattern he set. The Antichrist's will is real, and his evil is real, and neither of them is in charge of the calendar.

> **Key Principle:** Everything in Revelation happens on God's initiation and by God's timing. The seals are not Satan's campaign that God is countering — they are God's plan, in which Satan's agent is permitted a defined role for a defined period.

That has to be settled here, at the first seal, because it governs the rest of the book. Every judgment that follows is announced from a throne before it is executed on the earth.

And there is a purpose behind the permission. The point of what follows is not comfort and it is not punishment for its own sake. When suffering comes, people cry out to God. The Lord is allowing something like hell on earth so that people will turn to him before hell becomes permanent.

## 6. The Bow: Ezekiel 39 and the Same Rider at the Other End

One further piece of evidence identifies the rider on the white horse, and it comes from a passage this study has already covered.

Ezekiel 38 and 39 open with the Lord addressing [[Glossary#Gog|Gog]] of Magog, the chief prince of Meshech and Tubal, and announcing that he will turn him around, put hooks in his jaws, and drag him to the mountains of Israel. The sovereign hand is explicit — God is the one causing the battle to occur.

Then Ezekiel 39:3: *"Then I will strike your bow from your left hand and make your arrows drop from your right hand."*

The same weapon. The same figure. But at the opposite end of the story.

In Revelation 6, the bow is what he is given as he rides out; the text does not even mention arrows, because the weapon is functioning as a symbol of the authority to conquer rather than as an inventory of equipment. In Ezekiel 39, the bow is struck from his hand and the arrows fall — the imagery of disarming, of being rendered ineffective in battle. That is the end of his campaign, at Armageddon, when he is about to be destroyed.

The bow given and the bow struck away are the two bookends of one career. He is effective because the Lord has authorized him to be effective, for exactly as long as the Lord intends.

---

## 7. One King, Not Four

A natural reading of the four horsemen is that they are four different rulers doing four different evil things. They are not. They are four facets of one campaign.

Two things establish this. The first is internal: the rider is nameless in every case, and the only variables between the four scenes are the color of the horse and what that rider brings. There is no differentiation of person, only of effect.

The second is external, and it comes from Daniel. There is no prophetic warrant anywhere for multiple significant rulers in this period. Daniel gives one — and shows exactly how he gets there.

Both of Daniel's parallel visions, the statue of chapter 2 and the beasts of chapter 7, show ten kings arising first: the ten toes of iron and clay, and the ten horns on the fourth beast. Out of that group a [[Glossary#Four Kingdoms of Daniel|little horn]] rises. He subdues three of the ten by force. The remaining seven, seeing what happened to the three, hand their power over to him voluntarily. He becomes king over all ten kingdoms.

That is the two-step answer to how the Antichrist becomes king at all — and it has to happen before he can stand in the sanctuary and declare himself God. He has to hold the throne before he can claim divinity from it.

Which three does he conquer first? Daniel 11 and Ezekiel together name them. The [[Glossary#King of the North / King of the South|king of the South]] — Egypt — comes against him, and the king of the North destroys Egypt and seizes its treasures, along with its allies, Libya and Cush. Egypt, Libya, and Sudan are the three of the ten that fall by force.

Once the ten have handed over their power, they drop out of the narrative entirely. Their only prophetic function was to transfer authority. After that, Scripture identifies no other significant ruler in this period. There is a false prophet, but he works directly for the Antichrist — an agent, not a rival.

*(The false prophet will be taken up in a later session.)*

So when the second, third, and fourth horsemen ride out, they are not new claimants. They are what the first one brings with him.

## 8. The Second Seal: War, and the Covenant with Death

*"Then another horse came out, a fiery red one. Its rider was given power to take peace from the earth and to make men slay each other. To him was given a large sword."* (Revelation 6:4)

A conqueror bent on conquest produces war. That is not a second event; it is the consequence of the first.

He is the chief architect of every war in this period. There will be opposition to him, but he is the one winning, and he consolidates power continuously until the seventieth week runs out.

Which raises the question the covenant itself invites: if he arrives as a peacemaker, confirming a covenant with many to bring peace to the Middle East — is that peace real, and does it last?

Israel has been here before. Facing Assyria, Judah sought protection from Egypt rather than from the Lord. Later, facing Babylon, Hezekiah received Babylonian envoys and showed them everything in the treasuries and the house of the Lord — and Babylon came back, took the city, and carried the treasures away. The prophets have a name for arrangements like these. Isaiah calls them a [[Glossary#Covenant with Death|covenant with death]]: *"We have entered into a covenant with death, with the realm of the dead we have made an agreement... your covenant with death will be annulled; your agreement with the realm of the dead will not stand"* (Isaiah 28:15, 18).

The pattern is exact and it is about to repeat on the largest possible scale. A nation that will not trust the Lord for its security signs with whoever offers it, and the guarantor becomes the destroyer. The covenant with the Antichrist is the last covenant with death, and it ends the way the others did.

## 9. The Third Seal: Scarcity, Inflation, and Who Starves

*"I looked, and there before me was a black horse! Its rider was holding a pair of scales in his hand. Then I heard what sounded like a voice among the four living creatures, saying, 'A quart of wheat for a day's wages, and three quarts of barley for a day's wages, and do not damage the oil and the wine!'"* (Revelation 6:5–6)

This is famine, and it arrives in a specific form: ruinous inflation. A day's wages buys a day's ration for one person. You work a full day to eat, and there is nothing left over for anyone else in the house.

The mechanism is ordinary and entirely predictable. Nation rises against nation, as Matthew 24 puts it. War produces scarcity. Scarcity produces rationing, hoarding, and seizure. Prices rise on whatever is scarce. The wealthy absorb the increase and keep eating. The poor do not. The scales in the rider's hand are the instrument of a world where food is weighed out by the ounce and priced beyond reach.

The instruction not to damage the oil and the wine sharpens it further. Luxury goods are untouched while staples are rationed — a portrait of an economy in which the comfortable barely feel what is starving everyone else.

The rider is a symbolic mechanism rather than a literal agent handing out scales. But the Antichrist is the ultimate cause. War is his campaign, and famine is what war does.

---

## 10. The Fourth Seal: Death and Hades

*"I looked, and there before me was a pale horse! Its rider was named Death, and Hades was following close behind him. They were given power over a fourth of the earth to kill by sword, famine and plague, and by the wild beasts of the earth."* (Revelation 6:8)

This is the sum of the first three — the aggregate result of a conqueror, a war, and a famine.

The name is not a change of identity. The rider is still symbolic of the Antichrist and what his arrival produces. *Death* names the effect: human beings killed on a massive scale. [[Glossary#Hades|Hades]] follows behind because death delivers those who die apart from God somewhere.

That somewhere is what Hades names. Hades, hell, the lake of fire, the abyss — these are all words for the same reality: the place of eternal torment away from God. Scripture approaches it from different angles and with different vocabulary, but it is describing one destination, and the fourth seal is the mechanism that sends people there in numbers.

The scale is worth pausing on. There are roughly eight billion people on the planet. A fourth of that is two billion. For comparison, the Second World War — the deadliest event in recorded human history, including the industrialized killing carried out by the Third Reich — produced deaths in the tens of millions. There has never been an event on this planet in which two billion people died. That is the order of magnitude of what the fourth seal describes. It will not be possible to live through this period and not notice it.

## 11. The Fifth Seal: The Souls Under the Altar

The fifth seal is the hinge of the chapter, and its language has to be held carefully because it is the key to reading Revelation 7.

*"When he opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of those who had been slain because of the word of God and the testimony they had maintained... Then each of them was given a white robe, and they were told to wait a little longer, until the number of their fellow servants and brothers who were to be killed as they had been was completed."* (Revelation 6:9, 11)

Nothing in this passage names a time period. But the sequence can be reconstructed from what the rest of Scripture has already established.

These souls were martyred — killed for their testimony. Who is on the scene killing people for their testimony? The Antichrist. And what does Daniel, Matthew 24, and 2 Thessalonians say is the seminal event of the seven years? The [[Glossary#Abomination of Desolation|abomination of desolation]] at the midpoint, when he stands in the sanctuary and declares himself God. What does that event inaugurate? The great tribulation. And what characterizes the great tribulation? The saints are persecuted and killed.

So the fifth seal sits inside the great tribulation. And because these martyrs have *already* died while more are still to follow, the scene is somewhere in the middle of it — not at its start and not at its end.

Why doesn't John say so directly? Because he does not have to. When Jesus described the abomination of desolation to his disciples, his instruction was to go read Daniel — *let the reader understand.* He had already told them everything they needed. The Lord showing this vision to John knows that John knows Daniel, and expects the same of anyone reading. Very few places in Scripture lay everything out in one passage. This is one of the pieces, and it assumes you have the others.

There is one methodological consequence worth stating plainly: the fifth seal supplies no timing markers, so no timing can be extracted from it. The chronology has to come from elsewhere. That will be true at many points through the rest of Revelation.

## 12. Patient Endurance

The martyrs' cry — *how long, Sovereign Lord, holy and true, until you judge the inhabitants of the earth and avenge our blood?* — is not a complaint. Look at what is packed into the address. They call him sovereign. They call him holy and true. They are not questioning whether he will act. They are asking when.

And the answer they receive is not a date. Each is given a white robe, and they are told to wait a little longer, until the full number is complete. They are not given a task. They are given a garment and an instruction to wait.

There is a parallel structure here to something the study has already covered. Just as there is a [[Glossary#Fullness of the Gentiles|fullness of the Gentiles]] — a full number who must come in before the Lord returns — there is a full number of martyrs in the great tribulation. Both are sovereign counts. Neither is disclosed.

This is why the vision includes the question at all. It is going to be on the lips of every saint alive during that period. John records it because the answer needs to be on record before the question is asked.

What John calls for in Revelation is [[Glossary#Patient Endurance|patient endurance]] — not endurance as gritted teeth, but a specific condition of the heart while enduring. And patient endurance requires two things. It requires knowing God, and it requires hope. You cannot patiently endure something if you do not know the character of the one who allowed it, and you cannot patiently endure it if you do not know how it ends.

> **Key Principle:** Patient endurance is not a personality trait. It rests on two things: knowing who God is, and knowing what he has planned. Remove either one, and what is left is either despair or the impulse to take matters into your own hands.

Both of those are supplied by exactly the material in front of us. Reading this, we know God wins. We know he has a plan and we know its shape. If he has a plan and he is all-powerful, he can be trusted — and being able to trust him is what makes it possible to endure injustice that is not going our way, in a world where believers are being killed unjustly.

It is more pleasant to be a [[Glossary#Pre-Tribulation Rapture|pre-tribulationist]]. That has to be conceded honestly. A doctrine in which the church exits before any of this begins is easier to hold, easier to preach, and easier to hear. It is also of a piece with the [[Glossary#Prosperity Gospel|health, wealth, and prosperity]] teaching it so often travels alongside. But the comfort of a position is not evidence for it.

## 13. Why a Loving God Allows the Church to Go Through It

If the church is present for the great tribulation, the obvious question is why a loving God would permit that. Several answers converge.

**Purification.** Suffering strips away what is not real. Pressure applied to faith reveals whether there is anything under it.

**Prayer and proximity.** What happens to people when they suffer is that they draw closer to the Lord. It is a consistent pattern, individually and historically.

**Forcing a decision.** Every human life comes down to one choice: God, or not. Accept or reject. Under ordinary conditions, an enormous number of people never make it. They grow up in church, they are surrounded by Christians, it is normal in their family and social world, and so they are in church — and none of that establishes that they have a personal relationship with Jesus Christ. When conditions become genuinely costly, the fence disappears. You cannot stay on it. Everyone has to pick a side.

That last one is the load-bearing answer, and it reframes the whole question. It does not sound loving from where we stand. From God's vantage point, ask which is more loving: to let a person drift comfortably through life and arrive at a permanent judgment they never seriously considered, or to force the question while there is still time to answer it. Is earthly suffering worth eternal salvation? Stated that way, it answers itself. But it only answers itself if you are looking from God's side of it. From a purely human vantage point, the calculation cannot be made — you will choose comfort, or the wealth of this world, or the illusion of it, every time.

The pattern is not new. The father of the prodigal son is often read as a poor father — he hands over the inheritance and lets the boy go do whatever he wants. But look at the result. The son came back. Life happened to him, and life taught him what a lecture never could. Paul does the same thing structurally in 1 Corinthians 5, instructing the church to hand the unrepentant man over to Satan. The point is not abandonment. The point is releasing someone into the world as it actually is — full of evil and full of consequences — so that the consequences can do what argument cannot.

> **Key Principle:** Whether God is loving in permitting evil and suffering to exist depends entirely on what those things accomplish. If suffering brings people to God, it is loving. That is a conclusion only reachable from God's vantage point.

This is what a [[Glossary#Biblical Worldview|biblical worldview]] is. Not an American worldview, not a human worldview, not a progressive worldview — a framework in which you see events from God's perspective, because that perspective is the one he has revealed.

## 14. The Sixth Seal: The Day of the Lord

*"I watched as he opened the sixth seal. There was a great earthquake. The sun turned black like sackcloth made of goat hair, the whole moon turned blood red, and the stars in the sky fell to earth, as late figs drop from a fig tree when shaken by a strong wind. The sky receded like a scroll, rolling up, and every mountain and island was removed from its place."* (Revelation 6:12–14)

This is the most familiar language in the chapter, and it is familiar because it is everywhere. Isaiah, Joel, Ezekiel, and Zechariah all describe it. Jesus describes it in Matthew 24. In the prophets, this celestial event has a name: the [[Glossary#Day of the Lord|Day of the Lord]].

And in Matthew 24, what follows immediately is not ambiguous. *Immediately after the distress of those days*, the sign of the Son of Man appears in the sky — the [[Glossary#Shekinah Glory|Shekinah glory]], the cloud of God's presence — and he sends out his angels to gather his elect. The dead in Christ rise first; those still alive are caught up after them. That is the [[Glossary#Rapture|rapture]], and it lands directly on the far side of the sixth seal.

Why does the Lord darken the heavens right before he appears? To make certain that no one misses it. Jesus compares his coming to lightning visible from east to west, and to the sun coming up — you do not have to be told it happened. The theatrical logic is exact: before the first scene, the house lights go down, and then the spotlight comes on. Every eye in the room is already pointed at the one place where light is about to appear.

There is also a hard constraint buried in the imagery, and it does real work on the timeline. If the sun, the moon, and the stars go dark, the earth's source of heat is gone. Temperature drops immediately. The atmosphere begins to change. Whether the cause is cosmic or something like volcanic obscuration, humanity cannot survive that condition for long. Days, not months.

Which means the interval between the sixth seal and the appearing of the Son of Man is short. It cannot be an extended period. That constraint will matter when the timeline is assembled.

## 15. The Wrath of the Lamb

*"Then the kings of the earth, the princes, the generals, the rich, the mighty, and every slave and every free man hid in caves and among the rocks of the mountains. They called to the mountains and the rocks, 'Fall on us and hide us from the face of him who sits on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb! For the great day of their wrath has come, and who can stand?'"* (Revelation 6:15–17)

The list is comprehensive on purpose — kings and slaves, generals and free men. This is everyone on earth.

They are hiding because they have seen him. Not because of a vague dread, and not merely out of guilt over sin in the abstract. They have seen the face of the one seated on the throne, and in that instant every pretense, every alternative belief, everything they built a life on renouncing, evaporates. They see Christ returning in power and glory, and they know they chose the wrong side. They would rather be crushed under a mountain than face what is in the sky.

This is what "like a thief in the night" actually means. He comes as a thief to those who are not watching and not expecting him. He does not come as a thief to those who know the sequence.

And then the phrase that resolves the whole question of the church's placement: *the great day of their wrath has come.*

Paul is explicit that the church is not appointed to wrath — *God did not appoint us to suffer wrath but to receive salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ* (1 Thessalonians 5:9). Is the church subject to persecution? Yes. To tribulation? Yes. To wrath? No, because there is no condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.

> **Key Principle:** Tribulation and wrath are two different things with two different authors. The church passes through the great tribulation, which the Antichrist brings. The church does not pass through the wrath of God, which God brings.

So the logic runs in one direction only. If the wrath of God has arrived, and the Lord is visibly in the clouds, then the gathering of the church is not a distant event. It is the next one.

## 16. The Sealing of the 144,000

Revelation 7 does not advance the seal sequence. It stops it — and inserts two scenes that have to occur in the interval between the sixth seal and the seventh.

*"After this I saw four angels standing at the four corners of the earth, holding back the four winds of the earth to prevent any wind from blowing on the land or on the sea or on any tree. Then I saw another angel coming up from the east, having the seal of the living God. He called out in a loud voice to the four angels who had been given power to harm the land and the sea: 'Do not harm the land or the sea or the trees until we put a seal on the foreheads of the servants of our God.'"* (Revelation 7:1–3)

The urgency is the point. The great tribulation has been running for some time already. What is about to begin is different: the wrath of God. The angels are standing ready to harm the land, the sea, and the trees, and they are held back for one reason — a group has to be marked first so that what is coming does not touch them.

*"Then I heard the number of those who were sealed: 144,000 from all the tribes of Israel"* — twelve thousand from each tribe.

Who are they? Jews. Traditional Israel. Not the church, and not Christians.

That identification follows necessarily from where they are. If these were Messianic Jews — Jews who had received Christ — then when the Lord returned to gather his church, they would have been gathered with it. They are still on the earth. Therefore they had not believed at the time of the gathering.

Revelation 14 fills in the rest of the portrait. There they stand with the Lamb on Mount Zion with his name and his Father's name written on their foreheads. They sing a new song before the throne that no one else can learn. They are described as those who did not defile themselves with women, who kept themselves pure, who follow the Lamb wherever he goes, who were purchased from among men and offered as [[Glossary#First Fruits|first fruits]] to God and to the Lamb, in whose mouths no lie was found, and who are blameless. They are all men. And the fact that they appear in Revelation 14 standing on the earth with the Lamb tells you the sealing worked: every one of them survives the wrath of God.

*(The full treatment of Revelation 14 and the identity of the [[Glossary#144,000|144,000]] comes later in the study.)*

The sealing has clear precedent. In Ezekiel 9, before God sends out the executioners against Jerusalem, he sends a man with a writing kit to mark the foreheads of everyone who grieves and laments over the abominations being done in the city — and the destroyers are instructed not to touch anyone bearing the mark. The structure is identical: mark first, then judge.

The deeper precedent is Egypt. When the plagues came down on Egypt, Israel was in Goshen and Israel was protected. Whether it was the darkness, or the death of the firstborn, or any of the others, the Jews watched the power of God dismantle the greatest empire on earth without suffering the consequences of it themselves. That is exactly what the 144,000 will do. They will witness the wrath of God poured out on mankind, they will live through it, and they will carry that testimony to their brothers in Jerusalem.

Which is the reason for the whole arrangement. They are the [[Glossary#Millennial Kingdom|millennial kingdom]]'s first fruits — the beginning of the harvest of Israel, preserved through the judgment so that there is a nucleus in the land when the King takes his throne in Jerusalem.

## 17. The Great Multitude Out of the Great Tribulation

The second scene in Revelation 7 is the one that settles the timeline.

*"After this I looked, and there before me was a great multitude that no one could count, from every nation, tribe, people and language, standing before the throne and before the Lamb. They were wearing white robes and were holding palm branches in their hands."* (Revelation 7:9)

Set this against the 144,000 immediately preceding it. Those were from the tribes of Israel, twelve thousand each, counted precisely. This [[Glossary#Great Multitude|great multitude]] is from every nation, tribe, people, and language, and no one can count it. Two different groups, deliberately contrasted.

And set it against the souls under the altar in the fifth seal. Those were *souls*, and they were *given* white robes. These are wearing the robes and holding palm branches — and a soul cannot wear a robe or hold a branch. Between the fifth seal and this scene, they have received [[Glossary#Resurrection Body|resurrection bodies]]. The resurrection has occurred.

They are standing before the throne and before the Lamb, which places them in heaven.

Then John records the exchange that removes the last ambiguity. One of the elders asks him: *"These in white robes — who are they, and where did they come from?"* John defers, and the elder answers his own question: *"These are they who have come out of the great tribulation; they have washed their robes and made them white in the blood of the Lamb."*

There is nothing clearer than that. The multitude standing in heaven in resurrection bodies, before the seventh seal is broken, is identified by heaven itself as having come out of the great tribulation. They were in it when the Lord came for them.

The promises that follow are the closing of a very long loop. They are before the throne of God and serve him day and night in his temple. He who sits on the throne will spread his tent over them. Never again will they hunger; never again will they thirst. The sun will not beat down on them, nor any scorching heat. The Lamb at the center of the throne will be their shepherd; he will lead them to springs of living water; and God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.

That is the answer to *how long*. Not a date — a destination.

## 18. The Timeline Assembled

With Revelation 6 and 7 in hand, the sequence can be stated in order.

The first seal is the covenant with many, which starts the seven years. The first four seals all fall in the first half, before the midpoint. The fifth seal falls after the abomination of desolation, inside the great tribulation, at a point where martyrs have already died and more are still to come. The sixth seal is the Day of the Lord — the darkening of the heavens — followed within days by the appearing of the Son of Man and the gathering of the church.

Then, before the seventh seal: the 144,000 are sealed, and the great multitude appears in heaven.

The seventh seal initiates the [[Glossary#Seven Trumpets|seven trumpets]]. The seventh trumpet initiates the [[Glossary#Seven Bowls of Wrath|seven bowls of wrath]]. The seventh bowl culminates in the Battle of Armageddon, the final judgment, and the death of the Antichrist — which ends the seventieth week.

*(The trumpets, the bowls, and Armageddon will each be taken up in their own sessions.)*

This is also where Matthew 24:22 lands. Jesus said that if those days had not been cut short, no one would survive. This is the Lord keeping that promise. Somewhere in the middle of the great tribulation, when the full number of those to be martyred for their testimony is complete, he returns, takes his church home, seals the 144,000, and breaks the seventh seal.

One argument settles why the return cannot be placed as late as the seventh trumpet, which some pre-wrath readings propose. The fifth and sixth trumpets have an explicit duration: the demonic army released in Revelation 9 is given five months to torment mankind. That is not an inference — the text states it twice. If the Lord's return were at the seventh trumpet, the sun, moon, and stars of the sixth seal would have to remain dark across those five months and everything before them. No human being would be alive to be gathered. It is not possible. The sixth seal and the gathering have to be adjacent.

What happens after the gathering is that the church goes to heaven first. The great multitude is standing before the throne, not on the earth. The wedding feast of the Lamb occurs there. The Lord brings the church back with him when he returns for Armageddon, stands on the Mount of Olives, and marches on Zion.

> **Key Principle:** The Lord does not come as a thief in the night to those who know the sequence. We do not know the day. We know the season, we know the event that immediately precedes his coming, and we know the events that precede that one.

## 19. Why He Tells Us Any of This

There is an obvious objection to the entire chapter. After Revelation 7, the church is in heaven. The remaining fourteen judgments are executed on an earth we are not standing on. So why does the Lord give us this level of detail about events we will not experience?

Because of the question in the fifth seal.

*How long, Sovereign Lord, until you judge the inhabitants of the earth and avenge our blood?* That question gets asked by the martyrs, and it will be asked by every believer alive in that period. And the Lord's answer is not a date. His answer is the rest of the book — a complete account of exactly how he intends to bring justice to the earth and bring it to a conclusion.

Ask whether it is easier to wait, to trust, and to patiently endure when you know precisely how the Lord is going to act. It is. It is the difference between believing that God will eventually do something and knowing what he has already scheduled. That knowledge is what removes the impulse to take matters into our own hands, or to fight against a plan we do not recognize as a plan.

It will not be easy to go to prison. It will not be easy to be martyred. The only way through that is knowing what comes next and who has arranged it. Which is why, in his grace, he has given us the plan. A substantial share of the pages of Scripture are devoted to telling us what he intends to do before he does it. He wants us to know.

And the last word on it is a matter of where the attention goes. By the time the trumpets have run their course, more than half the population of the earth is dead. But the point of Revelation is not the events. The point is the God being worshipped in the middle of them. He is King of the universe, King of kings and Lord of lords — and he is also, personally, our Lord and Savior. Though we walk through the valley of the shadow of death, we fear no evil, because he is with us.
