### Q: What would you say to someone who believes Revelation is entirely symbolic, and that the events it describes — however terrible — are not going to actually happen?

The right response starts by asking what the rest of biblical prophecy actually looks like. Roughly a quarter of Scripture is prophetic, and where that prophecy has already been fulfilled — in Daniel, most clearly — it was fulfilled literally, even in passages built on vivid symbolic imagery. Daniel's symbolic sections come with their own interpretation attached; the vision is symbolic, but the interpretation tells you the vision points to real, literal events. There is no reason to expect Revelation to operate under different rules. Ultimately, though, whether someone accepts this is a matter of what they are already committed to believing. Someone who holds the Quran as final authority is not going to be persuaded by an appeal to what the Bible says, because they don't grant the Bible's authority to begin with. The evidence for a literal reading is available to anyone who traces the symbolism back to its biblical source — but most people who reject that approach are not doing so because the evidence is unclear. They are doing so because they have not looked.

### Q (Audience contribution): The seven horns and seven eyes are both connected to "the seven spirits of God" in the text. Could that mean both images together are combined imagery for the Holy Spirit, rather than the horns meaning something separate?

That's a reasonable way to read the wording, and it's not wrong — but it's also not the whole picture. Horns carry established meaning elsewhere in Scripture as symbols of authority, kings, and kingdoms, most notably in Daniel's vision of the beasts. Whether the seven horns are read as a reference to the sevenfold Spirit or as a symbol of complete authority, the conclusion lands in the same place: the Lamb has full and complete authority over the kingdom of heaven, exercised through the church. The seven eyes, more straightforwardly, describe the Spirit's complete sight and presence sent into all the earth. The two images work together without needing to collapse into a single referent.

---

### Q: If Jesus already has full authority over heaven, why does he still need to open the scroll — and hasn't Satan not yet been cast out at this point in the vision?

These are two separate kinds of authority, and the vision keeps them distinct on purpose. Jesus has established, uncontested authority over the kingdom of heaven now — that authority is real and complete, and nothing in the scene suggests otherwise. What he does not yet have is realized authority over the earth. Satan has not yet been cast out of heaven at this point in Revelation's timeline; that removal comes later, in Revelation 12. Satan's authority over the physical, earthly realm remains active until the scroll's seals accomplish what they are set apart to accomplish. Christ's authority in the spiritual realm is already absolute — Satan can accuse, but he cannot compel God or Christ to do anything — but the same authority has not yet been made visible and effective in the physical world. The scroll is the mechanism that closes that gap.

### Q: Is it correct that a third of the angels rebelled and fell with Satan?

That detail belongs to Revelation 12, and it will be addressed fully when that chapter is reached.

### Q: What does it actually mean to meditate on a passage of Scripture?

Meditation is not a single act — it's a cycle. Read the passage. Think about it. Read it again. Pray over it, and specifically ask the Holy Spirit to illuminate what it means, since he is the one who reveals it. Write down initial impressions. Come back to it later — sleep on it if needed — and read it again with fresh eyes. The most important discipline inside that cycle is reading for what the text actually says rather than what you already expect it to say. Whatever a reader already believes to be true is what dominates how they interpret anything they read, which means an unexamined bias will simply confirm itself every time. Reading with that awareness, and asking the Lord directly what he intends by what he has written, is what separates meditation from passive familiarity.
