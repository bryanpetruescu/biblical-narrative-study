## Apocalyptic Literature: How Literal Events Get Symbolic Clothing

The interpretive question the session opens with — how much of Revelation is literal and how much is symbolic — is really a question about genre. Revelation identifies itself in its opening verse as an *apokalypsis*, an "unveiling," and it belongs to a recognized body of ancient Jewish literature that includes Daniel, large portions of Ezekiel and Zechariah, and extra-biblical works such as 1 Enoch. Apocalyptic writing has a set of recurring conventions: a human seer is given a vision, often escorted or addressed by an angelic figure; the vision employs symbolic creatures, numbers, and cosmic imagery; and the symbolism is typically resolved either by an explicit interpretation given within the text or by imagery that would have been immediately recognizable to a reader steeped in earlier Scripture.

Daniel 7 is the clearest internal model for how this works. Daniel sees four beasts rise from the sea — imagery as strange as anything in Revelation — and then the vision itself supplies the key: "these four great beasts are four kings" (Daniel 7:17), later clarified as four kingdoms. The vision is symbolic. What it depicts is not. This is the pattern apocalyptic literature consistently follows, and it is the reason careful interpreters treat Revelation's imagery as pointing to real, historical realities rather than functioning as a self-contained symbol system that means whatever a given reader wants it to mean. The genre invites symbolic reading of the *imagery* precisely because it expects literal fulfillment of what the imagery describes.

## The Throne Room Vision: Ezekiel 1 and the Cherubim

John's vision of the throne draws directly on Ezekiel's inaugural vision in Ezekiel 1, written roughly six centuries earlier during the Babylonian exile. Ezekiel describes four living creatures, each with four faces — human, lion, ox, and eagle — beneath a throne of sapphire, surrounded by brilliant light "like the appearance of a rainbow in the clouds" (Ezekiel 1:28). The correspondence to Revelation 4 is not coincidental; John is seeing, and describing, the same reality Ezekiel saw, and the shared imagery signals continuity between the two visions rather than two unrelated events.

The creatures themselves are cherubim, a class of angelic being attested from the earliest chapters of the Hebrew Bible. Genesis 3:24 places cherubim with a flaming sword at the entrance to Eden after the expulsion of Adam and Eve, guarding the way to the tree of life. Exodus 25:18–20 has two golden cherubim, their wings overshadowing the mercy seat, forming the throne imagery atop the Ark of the Covenant — the physical furniture of the tabernacle deliberately modeled on the heavenly throne room itself. Ezekiel 28:14–16, in an oracle addressed to the king of Tyre but widely read as describing the fall of Satan, identifies the figure being denounced as "the anointed cherub who covers," present in Eden, until iniquity was found in him. Cherubim, in other words, are not generic angels; they are specifically associated with guarding sacred space and standing in closest proximity to God's own presence — which is exactly the function they perform in both Ezekiel's and John's visions.

## Sealed with Seven Seals: The Legal Document Convention

The description of the scroll in Revelation 5 — written on both sides, sealed with seven seals — reflects a documentary practice with clear precedent in the ancient world. A scroll written on both sides (*opisthograph*, in the technical term) was unusual enough to signal that a document contained an especially large or especially important amount of content, since scribes normally wrote only on the inner side of a papyrus or parchment roll. Multiple wax or clay seals affixed by witnesses were a recognized feature of formal legal instruments across the Greco-Roman world, particularly wills and testaments. Roman law from this period required a valid will to bear the seals of seven witnesses — a practice attested in surviving papyri and in Roman legal sources describing testamentary procedure. A document of this kind could not be opened, and its contents could not take legal effect, until someone with the standing to do so broke each seal in turn.

Read against that background, the scroll in Revelation 5 functions precisely as a legal instrument of inheritance — a testament whose contents cannot be executed until it is properly and lawfully opened. The angel's question, "Who is worthy to break the seals and open the scroll?" is not a poetic flourish. It is a real legal question about standing, and the answer — that only the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, is qualified — identifies Jesus as the sole rightful heir to whatever the document conveys.

## Lion of the Tribe of Judah, Root of David: Messianic Titles

Both titles applied to Jesus in Revelation 5:5 have deep roots earlier in the Hebrew Bible. "The Lion of the tribe of Judah" draws on Jacob's deathbed blessing over his son Judah in Genesis 49:9–10: "Judah is a lion's cub... The scepter will not depart from Judah, nor the ruler's staff from between his feet, until he to whom it belongs shall come." Judah is identified as the tribe from which kingship — and ultimately the Messiah — would arise, a promise that runs forward through David and into the New Testament's genealogies of Jesus.

"The Root of David" draws on Isaiah 11:1 and 11:10 — "A shoot will come up from the stump of Jesse; from his roots a Branch will bear fruit... In that day the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner for the peoples." The image is deliberately paradoxical: a root does not come from a tree, a tree comes from a root. Applied to David's line, the title signals that the Messiah is simultaneously David's descendant and David's source — a claim consistent with the New Testament's insistence that Jesus is both fully human, born into David's line, and fully divine, existing before David and giving rise to him. Revelation 22:16, near the book's close, has Jesus apply this same title to himself directly: "I am the Root and the Offspring of David."
